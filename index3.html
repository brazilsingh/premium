The problem

Your AI agents are already running. Can you prove what they did?

AI agents are making decisions inside your business right now. Screening applicants, routing claims, answering customers, moving data. Most organisations cannot say what those agents did, which touched personal data, or what happened when an automation failed silently.

No visibility

Agents run, configurations change, conversations happen. Nobody sees it until something breaks.

No evidence

EU AI Act documentation assembled by hand, after the fact, in the week the auditor arrives.

No control

Nothing stops an agent doing something it shouldn't. You find out afterwards, from someone else.

EU AI Act compliance

Built for EU AI Act compliance, not adapted to it

Define your AI systems, including the connected infrastructure the Act brings into scope. Classify each as high-risk, limited-risk or prohibited. Then do the part most tools skip: map the specific harm scenarios and identify which groups those harms would fall on.

Systems that touch hiring, credit, healthcare or public services are high-risk by default under Annex III, with heavier reporting obligations. @Human generates that reporting from records it already holds.

Who does it protect?

Applicants filtered by a screening model. Patients triaged by an AI assistant. Children affected by content recommendations. Elderly people dependent on automated care decisions. The EU AI Act exists because these decisions have consequences, and someone has to be accountable.

FAQ

AI governance, answered

What is an AI governance platform?

An AI governance platform lets an organisation build, monitor and prove the compliance of its AI agents and automations. @Human logs every agent action, tracks failures as incidents, and maps each AI system to regulations like the EU AI Act, so compliance is demonstrable rather than assumed.

Is @Human EU AI Act compliant?

@Human is built for EU AI Act compliance. Its risk-management module classifies AI systems as high-risk, limited-risk or prohibited, maps harm scenarios to affected groups, and generates the documentation the Act requires.

Is @Human open source?

Yes. @Human is open source under the Apache 2.0 licence. You can inspect, modify, self-host and deploy it on your own infrastructure with your choice of LLM, including European providers such as Mistral.

Can @Human be self-hosted for data sovereignty?

Yes. @Human can be self-hosted on your own infrastructure or run in an EU cloud region. Your logs, audit records and sensitive data stay where you put them.
